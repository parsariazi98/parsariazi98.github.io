Physics-informed learning for perception systems that have to work outdoors.

My current work asks how far a camera can get toward knowing where it is from the polarization of the sky alone. The core problem is data: real polarimetric sky captures are expensive and geographically narrow, while the underlying optics are highly structured. I use that structure to synthesize training data, then evaluate under day-held-out protocols that keep the synthetic distribution out of validation and test entirely.

Earlier threads run through multimodal clinical generation, robot visual servoing, and IMU-based rehabilitation intelligence — all variations on the same interest in models that survive contact with real sensors.

Research interests

Three questions I keep returning to.

Physics as a data prior

When measurements are scarce but the forward model is known, simulation is not a fallback — it is a way of encoding the prior. The open question is how to close the remaining sensor gap without leaking synthetic structure into evaluation.

Honest evaluation protocols

Most of the difficulty in outdoor sensing is in the split, not the architecture. I design day-held-out and site-held-out protocols so that reported gains reflect generalization rather than temporal correlation.

Embodied and clinical sensing

Robot visual servoing, wearable IMU arrays, and multimodal patient records all impose constraints before the model is chosen: sensor placement, calibration, latency, and patient burden.

Current research · BioSensors Lab · Aug 2025 – present

Skypol: geolocalization from the polarization pattern of the sky.

A single polarimetric image of the sky encodes the sun's position through the Rayleigh scattering geometry, and the sun's position over a day encodes latitude and longitude. Skypol turns that chain into a learning problem, and spends most of its effort on the part that usually decides the outcome: where the training data comes from and how the test set is held out.

Why the data pipeline is the contribution

A physics simulator produces clean angle-of-polarization fields that are correct in geometry and wrong in everything else — noise, vignetting, saturation near the zenith, sensor response. Training on them alone transfers poorly. The emulation stage closes that gap without inventing data: each simulated frame is paired with a real capture at a comparable solar zenith, ranked by angular separation, and the real frame is rotated by the residual azimuth so its texture is preserved while its heading matches the simulated geometry.

  • Spherical-triangle sampling. Observers are drawn inside a geodesic triangle around the target site; the target itself is never simulated, so the model is asked to interpolate to a location it has not seen.
  • Trajectory supervision. Each day is represented by 64 samples spaced uniformly from sunrise to sunset (≈675 s apart), so the network sees a full solar arc rather than isolated frames.
  • Great-circle loss. The location head emits raw xyz, normalizes it to the unit sphere, and is trained with a cosine embedding loss — the loss angle is the great-circle position error directly, with no latitude/longitude parameterization artifacts.
  • Heading as an auxiliary signal. Adding heading to the trajectory predictor reduced error in both simulation and real data; a separate head is trained with MSE on cos(zenith).

Pipeline

  • Vision Transformer over polarization frames for solar-elevation trajectory prediction.
  • Two-stage PointTransformer for geolocation regression from the predicted trajectory.
  • Rayleigh forward model for angle- and degree-of-polarization synthesis.
  • Zenith-band matching and azimuth rotation for sensor-faithful emulation.

Protocol

  • Real campaign days Jul 1 – Aug 29, held out in chronological blocks.
  • ≈40% train / 30% validation / 30% test, with no day appearing in two splits.
  • Synthetic and emulated rows are retained only on real training days.
  • Validation and test contain real data exclusively.
3 Training regimes — real-only, simulation + real, emulation + real — scored on one shared evaluation set.

Intelligent Motion Lab · 2024

Robust 3D head pose estimation for visual servoing

A robotics perception pipeline sharpened by a Spring 2024 computer vision report on clinical head tracking, then extended toward multi-camera visual servoing.

  • Used 3DDFA_V2 with four selected landmarks and a UKF instead of relying on raw frame-to-frame detections.
  • Reported landmark tracking error under 5 pixels relative to 3DDFA_V2 on the BiWi Kinect dataset.
  • Exposed half-face poses as the hardest regime before integration into the broader UR5 visual servoing stack.

Sharif University of Technology · Journal paper

IMU-based telerehabilitation system design

A full system-design study for Parkinson's telerehabilitation that minimizes patient burden while preserving clinically useful recognition.

  • Optimized a 10-IMU setup down to a left thigh and left forearm pair.
  • Reached 95% recall on healthy-subject data and maintained above 80% after patient adaptation.
  • Structured the pipeline explicitly as data acquisition, optimal HAR design, and one-session PD adaptation.

Sharif University of Technology · Augmentation studies

Rehabilitation data augmentation studies

Two companion studies test augmentation from both sides: whether it improves HAR performance and whether the generated rehab motion still looks natural to experts.

  • Benchmarked six time-series augmentation methods from a 92% raw baseline up to 98% with jittering.
  • Used 6 IMUs, 21 therapeutic activities, and 18 expert raters to judge realism and variation.
  • Showed there is no one best augmentation method across all activity types, especially for dynamic walking tasks.
